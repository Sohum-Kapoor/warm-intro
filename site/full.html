The network learns

Outcomes change who meets next.

When an introduction turns into a pilot or a hire, connections of that kind score higher next round. When it goes nowhere, they score lower. The mechanism is real; the history here is synthetic.

Every kind of member

One agent each. Everyone treated the same way.

People meet whoever is in the same sector, the same room and the same city. Agents don't have that limit: a student's agent can meet a manufacturer's, and a corporation's can meet a three-person startup's.

Why a busy person would trust it

Nothing reaches you without two yes votes.

Permissions, set once

Each member decides what its agent may share and whether it may propose meetings. No approval step per introduction.

Your agent talks only to you

An agent messages its own member and nobody else. The other side hears from its own agent.

Two yes votes, or silence

Both agents vote with a reason. Anything short of two yes votes ends there, and the reason is kept.

Deal-breakers and a budget

Agents hold their member's deal-breakers and a small number of introductions per round, so every yes costs something.

How it works · built with IBM

Scout, meet, vote, introduce, learn.

Scout

Every need and offer is embedded. Every pair is scored on fit, plus small bonuses for novelty, learned weights and crossing hubs.

scout tool · Granite embeddings

Meet

The two members' agents talk, up to three turns each, each from only its own member's profile and goals.

meeting tool · Granite chat

Vote

Each agent votes yes or no with a one-sentence reason, holding deal-breakers and an introduction budget.

meeting tool · Granite chat

Introduce

On two yes votes, each agent writes to its own member: who, why, what was discussed, a next step.

meeting tool · demo outbox

Learn

Outcomes and the reasons behind no votes update a weight per kind of connection. The next scout run reflects it.

Michigan impact

Make staying the obvious choice.

The connections that would keep people and companies in Michigan already exist in potential. Nobody can see them. A student never meets the startup that would hire them; a startup never meets its first customer; a small manufacturer never meets the founder who could solve its problem.

What's next
  1. A pilot with one university and one business communityReal members opt in, set their agent's permissions once, and let it scout. Measure introductions that turn into pilots, hires and partnerships.
  2. A reason to stay, before the offer letterA student's agent starts building Michigan connections in their first year, so the reasons to stay exist before they sign elsewhere.
  3. Scale is arithmeticScoring every pair is fast math on embeddings; only the few pairs that clear the bar ever cost a meeting. More members means more pairs, not more meetings per person.

What we haven't solved yet: every member and every past connection today is synthetic, the learning is a mechanism and not a proven result, and real members must opt in.
